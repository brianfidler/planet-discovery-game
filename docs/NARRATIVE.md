# Narrative

## What this game needs (and doesn't)

Not a plot so much as a **frame, an arc, and an emotional throughline**. Like Outer
Wilds and Subnautica, the story should be *discovered through play*, not told in
cutscenes. Form rhymes with content: if the gameplay is about learning to see, the
story should be something the player gradually perceives, not something narrated.

## The spine (locked — holds regardless of tone)

A three-act arc already implied by every design decision:

- **Act I — Going out.** A scientist (not a soldier/colonist) crosses between stars
  to look for life. Learns to find the first hidden life; meets Halley.
- **Act II — Learning to see.** Life is nearly everywhere, but perception is the
  limit. Halley starts confident, gets humbled, learns alongside the player. Tiers of
  life unlock as senses expand. The universe fills in.
- **Act III — Coming home.** Return to Earth and finally perceive the unrecognized
  life that was always here. The alien perception aesthetic bleeds into the familiar
  handcrafted world — the climax rendered directly in the art.

The **player–Halley relationship is the emotional spine**: two minds, one human and
one artificial, discovering how little they understood.

## The open decision: which key is it played in?

This is the keystone. One frame should dominate; they can blend, but one is the
governing key.

### Option A — Golden age / pure wonder
Humanity has solved its big problems; you explore from abundance and curiosity.
Tone: awe, joy, discovery for its own sake. Earth reveal is a **gift** — "look how
much beauty was here all along." Gentlest, most hopeful; stakes internal/intellectual.

### Option B — Quiet necessity / elegiac
Earth isn't dramatically dying but is diminished — biodiversity thinned, something
lost. You go out partly seeking new homes, partly because we're realizing how little
we ever understood the life we had. Earth reveal turns **bittersweet** — you finally
see the unrecognized life that survived and grasp we destroyed things we never even
perceived. Tone: melancholy threaded with hope-through-humility. Heaviest
emotionally; the ethics layer becomes native here.

### Option C — The mystery
A question drives the journey — a signal, an anomaly, evidence that life is hidden
*for a reason*, or that something else is also looking. Exploration pulled forward by
unfolding mystery (Outer Wilds-style). Earth reveal is the final **twist**. Tone:
curiosity and intrigue building to wonder, with some unease. Most plot-propulsive.

### Current lean (held loosely — this call should be the developer's)
**B with a thread of C.** The elegiac frame gives the Earth reveal maximum punch and
answers the ethics question for free; a light mystery engine keeps it propulsive
instead of drifting into melancholy. But A is genuinely lovely — if the instinct is
"I want this hopeful," that's a valid, different game, not a lesser one.

## What resolving this unlocks

Tone determines stakes, progression feel, art mood, audio register, and the ethics
layer. Most of `OPEN-DECISIONS.md` is downstream of this one choice. Resolve the key,
and the rest largely falls out.

and the **opening sequence the vertical slice dramatizes**. (Deliberately deferred
