# Landing Page Copy

Working title placeholder: **Lifesigns** (alts: *The Unseen*, *Spectra*). Name is its
own decision — swap freely.

> Strategy note: a landing page with email signup (Carrd or similar) is the cheapest,
> lowest-risk way to validate the *pitch* before spending on a Steam page ($100 +
> review + media) or a Kickstarter (public failure risk). You own the email list,
> which is more valuable than wishlists. Drive a little traffic from where your
> audience already gathers, watch the signal (and its *tenor*), then invest if it's
> there. Caveat: concept interest ≠ finished-game interest, and weak art/copy can give
> a false negative — so presentation has to be genuinely good.

## Long form (landing page)

**HERO HEADLINE**
The universe is full of life. Almost none of it looks the way you'd expect.

**SUBHEADLINE**
A scientific exploration game about finding — and learning to recognize — alien life
across a universe of simulated worlds.

**THE PITCH**
Humanity can finally cross the gulf between stars. You go not as a soldier or a
colonist, but as a scientist. Your mission is to find life — and life, it turns out,
is nearly everywhere. The trouble is that it adapts to whatever world it's born on,
and most of it won't register on instruments built for Earth. A planet that reads as
dead may be quietly teeming. To find what's actually there, you'll have to invent new
ways of seeing, and question everything you assumed life had to be.

**WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT**

*Worlds that actually make sense.* Every planet, moon, and star is built from real
physics and chemistry, including elements that don't exist on our periodic table.
Nothing is random. A world is the way it is because of how it formed, what it's made
of, and the star it orbits. Understand the chemistry and you understand the world.

*Life you have to learn to see.* Biology adapts to extremes we can barely imagine:
life under crushing pressure, in poisonous air, on timescales so slow it looks like
geology. The right discovery is often a matter of building the right sense — a new
lens, a new instrument, a new hypothesis about what "alive" even means.

*Halley, your fallible partner.* Your only companion is an AI as far from home as you
are. Halley is brilliant, but it was trained on a universe it has now left behind, so
it guesses, it hedges, and sometimes it's confidently wrong. You'll test its theories
together and watch it learn alongside you.

*First contact, on life's terms.* Some of what you find can be communicated with.
Translation isn't a cutscene — it's a puzzle, an act of patience and inference, and
the most extraordinary thing the game has to offer.

**[teaser line, near the bottom]**
And the longer you spend learning to see life out there, the more you may start to
wonder what you've been missing back home.

**CALL TO ACTION**
Lifesigns is early in development. If a universe quietly full of life sounds like
somewhere you'd want to go, sign up below. I'll send occasional development updates,
concept art, and an early word when there's something to play.
*[ email signup field ]*

## Copy choices (rationale)
- Lead with the hook, not the features — "life is everywhere but unrecognizable" is the
  single strongest differentiator.
- Earth reveal stays a one-line tease — lands harder as a "wait, what?" than as a bullet.
- Tone curious and grounded, not epic/bombastic — matches the science-and-wonder audience.
- Terraforming and credibility/progression deliberately omitted to keep the pitch tight
  and wonder-focused (could live in a "learn more" section lower down).
