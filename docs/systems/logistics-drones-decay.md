# System: Logistics, Drones & Decay

## Cross-planet dependencies (the web)

A sensor for planet A may need a material from planet B. This turns isolated worlds
into a *connected web* — places with relationships — which is the antidote to hollow
proc-gen. Lean into it.

### Reconcile with local improvisation via a scarcity spectrum
- **Common** materials are everywhere → most routine tools improvised locally.
- **Rare/critical** materials are unevenly distributed → these drive cross-planet
  sourcing and the hardest worlds.
Local improvisation handles the day-to-day; scarcity handles the strategy.

### Avoid the fetch-quest trap
The thing players hate is *backtracking*. Rule: **dependencies push you outward into
the unexplored, not backward into the cleared.** You shouldn't *return* for a material;
you should *press on* somewhere new that happens to have it. Then every material run is
also a fresh expedition.

Prefer **discovery over directives** — no "fetch Element 119" quest marker. You survey,
build a picture of what's where, and *recognize* the connection yourself. Halley is
your fallible logistics memory ("I logged a possible source a few systems back, though
I wasn't sure of the concentration").

### Guardrails
- Keep **substitution** alive across planets — critical tools usually have several
  possible solutions from different places, so there's *a* route, not one mandatory
  pilgrimage.
- Avoid deep chains (B needs C needs D before A). One hop intrigues; three hops slog.

## Drones (autonomous logistics)

Dispatch drones to *previously visited* worlds to harvest identified materials while
*you* keep pressing into the unexplored. This is the clean resolution to backtracking:
return trips happen in the background as an idle management layer under forward
exploration.

### Connection to outposts/listening posts
Rather than hauling forever, leave automated extractors on resource-rich worlds that
supply you over time. Converts "go fetch" (chore) into "invest in infrastructure"
(strategy) — a quiet galaxy-spanning logistics network as a satisfying late-game
fantasy, and finally a mechanical reason for the listening posts from the original
pitch.

## Half-life / decay (timing as a planning puzzle)

Some compounds decay — you must harvest/use within a window. **Keep it a *planning*
puzzle, not a *reflex* one.** The challenge is routing and scheduling, not clicking
fast (a real-time stress clock fights the contemplative tone).

- Example: compound decays in 10 days; nearest source is a 7-day drone round-trip →
  margin exists; a farther source won't make it unless you *stabilize*, *process
  on-site*, or *stage a relay*.
- Tie instability to the **exotic, powerful** elements specifically (scientifically
  honest — heaviest synthetic elements have tiny half-lives; also a natural balancer
  against hoarding god-tier compounds).
- Implies a **stabilization tech branch** (slow decay, extend half-life, convert
  unstable→stable). More on-theme chemistry depth.
- Some compounds can't be stockpiled → just-in-time harvesting keeps logistics live.
- Decay is a property of *some* materials, not all. A special texture, not a universal
  stress clock.

## For the vertical slice

**Cut all of this** except one single hard-coded cross-planet dependency to prove the
"press onward to unlock" feeling. No drone network, no decay, no outposts yet. This is
a whole later phase.
