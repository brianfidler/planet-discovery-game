# System: Perception & Discovery

## The core fantasy

Your tools are your *senses*. The game's central verb is **see**, not make. Discovery
is gated by perception and hypothesis — not by travel or combat. The defining beat:
switch to the right sense, and life invisible a moment ago lights up.

## Sensors as senses (the "perceiving" tool family)

- Multispectral imager (IR/UV/beyond) — thermal life, non-visible signaling
- Spectrometer — composition from light alone
- EM-field detector — life that signals/senses electrically
- Temporal imager — life so slow it looks like geology until time is compressed
- Acoustic array — sound-based communication/movement
- Micro/nano-scope — the sub-visible

The full tool catalog (perceiving / analyzing / communicating / surviving-accessing)
lives in `materials-and-fabrication.md`.

## Sensor modes as a toggle, not a constant

Default view of a world is fairly grounded (solid procedural terrain you stand on).
**Life stays hidden until you apply the right sense** and the thermal/spectral/
schematic layer lights up. This:
- keeps stable ground underfoot,
- makes "switching senses to find life" an actual verb,
- turns the known/unknown contrast into something you *toggle*,
- gives a repeatable discovery beat — life snapping into view, every time.

## Tiered discovery (rarity on different timescales)

1. Trace biosignatures — common; most promising worlds have *something*.
2. Confirmed life of some kind — moderately common.
3. Complex life — uncommon.
4. Cognitive / communicative life — rare and memorable.
5. Novel biology that breaks Halley's current models — the grail.

Regular small wins; big moments stay rare. Each tier has its own emotional register.

## Detection rarity > presence rarity

The more powerful mechanic: make *detection* the rare thing, not *presence*. A world
that reads lifeless on first pass may not be — a later return with better tools and a
smarter Halley reveals what was missed. This yields replay value AND a slowly building
sense that the universe is full of life you couldn't see. Late-game players revisiting
early worlds and realizing what they overlooked (or terraformed over) is the
YouTube-clip moment.

## "No life found" must not feel like failure

A confirmed lifeless world is scientifically meaningful — it constrains your
astrobiology models and still has geology/chemistry/(later)terraforming value. Every
expedition produces value, just different kinds. Otherwise players treat dead worlds
as fail states and stop exploring.

## The Earth check-ins (the threaded payoff)

Non-mission-gated, atmospheric, optional visits home. As your toolkit and frameworks
grow, you notice new life in familiar places — extremophiles, plant chemical
signaling, octopus/corvid cognition. A persistent Earth log accumulates. Protect the
*noticing* feeling; the moment it becomes "complete the Earth checklist," it dies.
Builds toward the Act III climax where the alien perception aesthetic floods the
familiar world.

## For the vertical slice

One sensor mode beyond default, 3–4 hand-authored life forms across the discovery
tiers (a trace, a confirmed-simple, one surprising find). That's enough to prove the
north-star beat.
