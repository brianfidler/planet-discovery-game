# System: Materials, Fabrication & Tools

## Tools = senses & capabilities

Every tool either lets you *perceive* something, *understand* something, or *survive/
access* somewhere. That keeps the whole system pointed at the core fantasy instead of
sprawling into generic crafting.

### Four tool jobs
- **Perceiving** (the heart): multispectral imager, spectrometer, EM-field detector,
  temporal imager, acoustic array, micro/nano-scope.
- **Analyzing:** elemental assayer (incl. novel elements), core drill, atmospheric
  probe, Halley's hypothesis engine (the fallible interpretive overlay).
- **Communicating:** signal recorder/transceiver, Halley's translation matrix.
- **Surviving & accessing** (the exploration gate): tiered suits, shielding, remote
  drones for the truly hostile. You analyze a world, identify what would kill you
  (heat, pressure, radiation, corrosive atmosphere, an exotic element doing something
  strange), and can't set foot there until you've built protection.

## Fabrication = properties puzzle, not recipes

The trap is arbitrary recipes ("3×X + 2×Y = lens") — memorization that wastes the
scientific premise. The right model:
- The **tool** defines the *properties* it needs (e.g. "infrared-transparent AND
  cryo-stable housing").
- The **planet** offers a limited palette of materials with *simulated properties*.
- The **puzzle** is matching available materials to requirements — and dealing with
  what happens when you guess wrong.

### Halley's role in fabrication
Hand it an unknown material and it *hypothesizes* a use rather than knowing one.
Sometimes right; sometimes the result surprises both of you (occasionally better,
occasionally a problem). Crafting becomes discovery-under-uncertainty — on theme — and
novel elements become a thrill ("what will this become?") instead of a checklist.

### Difficulty curve for free
Early: Halley unsure about everything → puzzle-heavy crafting. As materials get
characterized → confident suggestions, low friction. Every new element/world
reintroduces uncertainty. Challenge concentrates at the frontier; no arbitrary level
gates needed.

### Don't make routine crafting tedious
Mirror real science: the *first* time you solve a recipe it's genuine discovery; after
that it's *known*, and Halley auto-suggests it whenever the right materials are
present. **Knowledge is permanent; only materials are consumed.** Puzzle-solving
concentrates on firsts and breakthroughs.

## Interface idea: drag-to-slots schematic

Instead of a crafting menu, show the tool as a schematic with slots that each *demand
a property*. Drag local materials into slots; the tool works fully / partially / fails
based on how well each material's real properties fit. Makes chemistry tactile and
failure legible ("the housing melted" teaches; a grayed-out button teaches nothing).

## Assist setting

A properties puzzle delights the Subnautica/Outer Wilds deduction crowd (your
audience) but can wall off players who want lighter crafting. Plan an assist mode:
Halley auto-suggests and auto-builds when confident. Depth rewards those who want it
without blocking those who don't.

## Materials tiers

- Common structural — basic gear, found everywhere, improvised locally.
- Energy materials — power, some exotic.
- Biological samples — sometimes reward, sometimes ingredient.
- **Novel elements (expanded periodic table)** — the source of "mind-bending tech."
  Real simulated properties determine what Halley can make; learning chemistry unlocks
  the tree. The most powerful tend to be the least stable (see decay doc).

## For the vertical slice

One or two tools fabricated via a simple properties-match; a handful of materials
(faked, not a full periodic-table sim); one genuine cross-planet dependency. Prove the
"match properties / guess under uncertainty" feel with minimal machinery.
